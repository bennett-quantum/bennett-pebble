# Pebble — formal core

Revision 2 (2026-11-10). Revision 1 was the working contract handed to the
theory, model and mechanisation tracks; they found twelve places where it was
false, imprecise or overstated (`docs/THEORY.md`, "Corrections to CORE";
`formal/README.md`, "Qualifications"). Every one is folded in below and marked
**[R2]**. Proofs: `docs/THEORY.md`. Machine-checked subset: `formal/`.
Executable evidence: `model/`.

The object of study: a compiler from a Bennett ISA 0.9 **lifted oracle**
(reference body + classical budget + classical arguments + target) to a
**permutation-tier circuit** realising the §5.2 contract, optimised for a
target objective under a peak-qubit budget. ISA references (`§n`) are to
`ref/isa-spec/spec/Bennett-ISA-Spec-0.9.md`.

Design loads, in order: (1) the Linux kernel under a step budget of 10⁹ and
megabytes of mostly-classical state; (2) SQLite; (3) Collatz
(`fixtures/collatz.bobj`) as the smoke-test baseline. Anything whose compile
time, compile memory or emitted description grows linearly in the budget B is
a design defect.

---

## 1. Setting

- **Wires** `W`, finite. Basis states `𝔹^W`. A **circuit** is a permutation of
  `𝔹^W` generated by `{X, CX, CCX, MCX}` (the permutation tier, north star
  §5.1). Its unitary is the permutation matrix, so T3 applies: agreement on
  basis states is exact agreement, no relative phase.
- **Environment** `x ∈ X`: one basis assignment to everything the oracle may
  read but not write (its `ins`, the cells of its `in` regions). Classical
  arguments, the budget tuple, the object and the target are fixed before
  compilation and are not part of `x`.
- **Reference function** `F_B : X → Payload × Code` (§5.2, §14.1).
- **Interface wires**: `R_in` (holding `x`), `tgt`, `fr`. Everything else is
  **scratch**.

## 2. Symbolic states and sound moves

**Definition 2.1 (symbolic state).** A symbolic state is a map
`κ : W_s → (X → 𝔹)` on scratch-plus-input wires `W_s = W ∖ (tgt ∪ fr)`: every
wire holds a known Boolean function of the environment. `enc_κ(x) ∈ 𝔹^{W_s}` is
the pointwise evaluation. A wire is **clean** in κ iff `κ(w) ≡ 0`, **constant**
iff `κ(w)` is a constant function. `space(κ)` is the number of non-clean
scratch wires. `κ₀` holds `x` on `R_in` and 0 elsewhere.
**[R2]** `space(κ)` counts information-bearing wires. It is not the physical
peak: an evaluator's clean target and transient workspace are live wires too.
Every space figure derived from the games below must add evaluator workspace
before it is a qubit count.

**Definition 2.2 (sound move).** A circuit `U` acting trivially on `tgt ∪ fr`
is a sound move `κ ⟶ κ′` iff `U(enc_κ(x)) = enc_κ′(x)` for every `x ∈ X`.

**Lemma 2.3 (algebra of moves).**
(a) *Composition*: `κ ⟶_U κ′` and `κ′ ⟶_V κ″` give `κ ⟶_{VU} κ″`.
(b) *Inversion*: `κ ⟶_U κ′` gives `κ′ ⟶_{U⁻¹} κ`.
(c) *Frame*: if `U` acts only on wires `A` and is sound from `κ|_A` to
`κ′|_A`, it is sound from `κ` to `κ[A ↦ κ′|_A]`.
(d) *Guarding*: if `U` is sound `κ ⟶ κ′` on `A` and `p ∉ A` is a wire with
`κ(p) = π`, then controlled-`U` on `p` is sound from `κ` to the state that is
`κ′` where `π(x)=1` and `κ` where `π(x)=0`, pointwise in `x`.

**Lemma 2.4 (staging).** Let `g` be a gate with control wire `c`.
If `κ(c) ≡ 0`, deleting `g` from a circuit preserves every sound move through
κ at that point; if `κ(c) ≡ 1`, so does deleting the control. Consequently a
wire whose content is constant *and which is used only through gates* never
needs to exist. (This is the soundness of partial evaluation of the reference
interpreter with respect to classical data.)
**[R2]** The consequence stops there. A read at a *superposed* address from a
classical image still needs a circuit computing that image's lookup function
over the address's value set, and a write at a superposed address makes the
cells it may touch non-constant. Classical state is free only where its
addresses are classical too.

**Theorem 2.5 (oracle contract from any sound compute).** Let
`κ₀ ⟶_U κ` with bundles `R_y, R_e` such that `κ(R_y)(x) = payload(F_B(x))` and
`κ(R_e)(x) = code(F_B(x))`. Let `K` be the commit: CNOT `R_y → tgt` bitwise,
CNOT `R_e → fr` bitwise. Then `V = U⁻¹ K U` satisfies, for all `x, t, f`:

    V |x⟩|t⟩|f⟩|0…0⟩ = |x⟩|t ⊕ payload(F_B(x))⟩|f ⊕ code(F_B(x))⟩|0…0⟩.

Moreover (i) `V` restricted to that domain is an involution; (ii) for a fresh
control wire `q`, `U⁻¹ (ctrl_q K) U = ctrl_q V` on that domain, so *only the
commit carries inherited controls*; (iii) the statement is independent of what
`κ` holds outside `R_y ∪ R_e`: garbage left by `U` is harmless, and cleanliness
matters only for wires `U` itself reuses.
**[R2]** Sharpened by the mechanisation: `V` is an involution on *every*
assignment, and `U⁻¹ (ctrl_q K) U = ctrl_q V` on every assignment; only the
contract itself is restricted to the clean-scratch domain. By linearity the
contract holds for inputs entangled with any reference system. For borrowed
dirty wires `d`: it suffices that the two result bundles equal `F_B(x)` for
*every* value of `d`; `V` then restores `d` exactly. Returning `d`'s basis
value is not enough if the answer depended on it.

Consequence for architecture: **the optimiser is outside the trusted base.**
A schedule is correct *if* it is a composition of certified sound moves ending
in the right two bundles; that is checked by replaying it in the rule calculus
of §3.
**[R2]** This is one direction only: not every correct circuit is derivable in
a fixed rule library. And "linear in the description" is proved for expanded
plays (the Lean checker); replaying a *compressed* schedule in time linear in
its compressed size needs the checker to understand the compression (shared
routines with wire-map parameters), which is specified in DESIGN and not yet
proved.

## 3. Named values and the rule calculus

Symbolic states are not directly checkable (function equality). The checkable
layer names values.

**Definition 3.1 (value system).** `𝒱 = (V, w, ⟦·⟧)` with widths
`w : V → ℕ` and denotations `⟦v⟧ : X → 𝔹^{w(v)}`. A **placement** is a finite
set `π` of pairs `(R, v)`, `R` a tuple of `w(v)` distinct scratch-or-input
wires, bundles pairwise disjoint. It denotes the symbolic state holding
`⟦v⟧` on `R` and 0 elsewhere. A value may appear at several bundles.

**Rules.** Each is a schema `π ⟷ π′` with a circuit; `Z` always denotes wires
clean in π.

| Rule | Semantic side condition | Placement change | Circuit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eval** `f` | `⟦v⟧ = f(⟦u₁⟧,…,⟦u_k⟧)` | `π ∋ (R_i,u_i)`: `π ⟷ π + (Z, v)` | XOR-form evaluator `E_f : ∣u⟩∣z⟩ ↦ ∣u⟩∣z ⊕ f(u)⟩`, self-inverse |
| **Copy** | – | `π ∋ (R,v)`: `π ⟷ π + (Z, v)` | CNOTs |
| **InPlace** `g` | `(⟦v⟧,⟦ρ⟧) = g(⟦u⟧, ⟦a⟧)` with `g(·, a)` injective for every `a` | `π ∋ (R_a, a)`: `π + (R,u) ⟷ π + (R′,v) + (R″,ρ)`, `R′ ∪ R″ = R ∪ Z` | bijective evaluator `I_g` |
| **Relabel** | the physical content of every wire is unchanged; only which bundle names which wires changes | same | none (zero gates) |
| **Guard** `p` | any rule above, `(R_p, p) ∈ π`, `w(p)=1` | values `v` become `p ? v : 0` (resp. `p ? v : u` in place) | the rule's circuit controlled on `R_p` |

**Theorem 3.2 (calculus soundness).** Every rule instance is a sound move
between the denoted symbolic states, provided its evaluator meets its
specification on the stated wires. Hence every derivation is.
**[R2]** Side conditions made explicit: operand, control and target bundles
are pairwise disjoint; stable operands are preserved by the evaluator;
`InPlace` needs a *specified permutation* on the full input and output layouts
(injectivity of `g` alone does not hand you the circuit); a guarded rule's
inverse must be taken under the same, unchanged predicate; `Relabel` is free
only as a change of interpretation — moving contents to fixed ABI wires is a
permutation circuit and costs gates.

**Corollary 3.3.** The reversible pebble game on a DAG (Bennett 1989) is the
`Eval`-only fragment: a pebbling strategy is a derivation. `InPlace` adds the
slide moves that make `x += y`, `x *= odd`, `x ^= y`, rotations, and
shift-with-residue cost no retained copy of the old value.

**Definition 3.4 (residue).** For an update `u ↦ v` of a dying value, a
**residue** is any `ρ` making `(v, ρ)` injective in `u` given the stable
operands. The always-available residue is `ρ = u` (out-of-place evaluation,
then relabel: the reference frame's displaced slot, §3.6). The compiler's job
in the data path is to choose rules minimising `w(ρ)`.

Residue table (stable operand `a`, width `n`, constant `c`):

| Update | Residue | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `u + a`, `u − a`, `u ⊕ a`, `u + c`, `¬u`, `rot(u, c)` | none | in-place adder / CNOTs / relabel |
| `u · c`, `c` odd | none | bijection mod 2ⁿ |
| `u ≫ c` (logical or arithmetic), `0 ≤ c < n` | low `c` bits of `u` | relabel, `c` fresh zero wires on top (arithmetic: plus sign fan-out) |
| `u ≪ c`, `0 ≤ c < n` | high `c` bits of `u` | relabel |
| any shift with `c ≥ n` | `u` | **[R2]** the ISA returns 0 for every shift by at least the width, `ashr` included |
| `trunc_k u` | dropped bits | relabel |
| `zext/sext u` | none | relabel (+ CNOT fan-out for sext) |
| `select(p, e₁(u), e₂(u))` | `p` (if `p` depends on `u`) ∪ guarded residues of `e₁, e₂` | arms in place under `p`, `¬p` |
| `e₂ ∘ e₁` | `ρ₁ ∪ ρ₂` | |
| anything else | `u` | out-of-place fallback |

A residue that is 0 whenever its guard holds may be dropped **[R2]** *when it
is the guarded (masked) residue*, i.e. the wire holds `p ? ρ : 0` and `ρ = 0`
under `p`, so it is 0 for every `x`. An unmasked wire that merely happens to
vanish on the active branch is not clean. Example: the bit shifted out of a
value by `≫ 1` inside the arm guarded by "value is even".

## 4. Budget

**Definition 4.1.** For fixed heap/stack/depth limits, let the *unbounded*
reference run on `x` execute `N(x) ∈ ℕ ∪ {∞}` rows up to and including its
halting row, with outcome `out(x) ∈ {ok(y), fault(code)}` when `N(x) < ∞`.

**Lemma 4.2 (budget truncation).** **[R2]** *Scope:* a prefix-stable family —
all non-step limits, classical arguments, layout, nested token tuples and
bindings fixed, only the enclosing `B` varying. The ISA guarantees this for
well-formed oracles. A nested `lift.oracle_call` is one outer row with its own
token's budget; inlining it must keep that separate budget and the single
outer charge.
`F_B(x) = (y, 0)` if `out(x) = ok(y)` and `N(x) ≤ B`;
`(0, code)` if `out(x) = fault(code)` and `N(x) ≤ B`;
`(0, FAULT_BUDGET_STEPS)` otherwise.

So the compiled machine may execute *more* than B rows, in any grouping,
provided it knows `N(x)` at the halt. The budget is a comparison, not a
schedule.

## 5. The guarded block machine

**Definition 5.1.** A guarded block machine (GBM) over state space `Σ` is
`(𝔅, δ, ω, halt)`: a finite set of blocks, a transition
`δ : 𝔅 × Σ → (𝔅 ∪ {HALT}) × Σ`, a row weight `ω : 𝔅 × Σ → ℕ_{≥1}`, and at
HALT an outcome register in Σ. It **simulates** the reference machine if, for
every `x`, its run from the initial state visits `(b_i, s_i)` such that the
reference run is the concatenation of segments of `ω(b_i, s_i)` rows, the
i-th segment taking the reference from the state abstracted by `s_i` to that
abstracted by `s_{i+1}`, and halting rows are last in their segment.

**Lemma 5.2.** Row merging into superblocks, inlining of lprocs (with the
`lift.call`/`lift.ret` rows kept in ω), if-conversion, and dropping of dead
locations preserve simulation, **[R2]** each under a certificate rather than
by its label: (i) call depth and stack faults are classical only when the
*dynamic* depth and stack pointer at the call are classically known, which a
known callee binding does not by itself give; (ii) dead-state removal is
justified by an observational relation (the removed state is never read
again), not by recoverability; (iii) if-conversion must suppress the faults
and effects of rows that the reference would not have executed, including
every row after the first fault.

**Theorem 5.3 (compiled function).** Let a GBM simulate the reference and let
`c` accumulate ω along its run, frozen at HALT. Let `H` be such that every
run not halted after `H` block steps has `c ≥ B` (`H = B` always works).
**[R2]** Revision 1 said `c > B`, which fails for a non-halting run of
weight-1 blocks (`c = B` exactly). `c` must be exact or saturate at `B + 1`;
modular wrap is unsound.
Running `H′ ≥ H` steps and committing

    y = payload if halted ∧ ok ∧ c ≤ B, else 0
    e = code if halted ∧ fault ∧ c ≤ B; FAULT_BUDGET_STEPS if ¬halted ∨ c > B; else 0

computes `F_B`. If, for every reachable `(t, b)`, `c` is a single classical
value, `c` needs no wires and over-budget transitions are pruned at compile
time.

**Definition 5.4 (time-indexed reachability).** `Reach₀ = {entry}`;
`Reach_{t+1} = ⋃_{b ∈ Reach_t} succ(b)`, computed classically (and tightened
by any sound classical analysis). Step `t` dispatches only `Reach_t`.
`Pred_t(b′) = {(b, e) : b ∈ Reach_{t−1}, exit e of b leads to b′}`.

**Construction 5.5 (predecessor-rank step).** State bundle
`σ = (pc, vars, [c], outcome)`; frame `ρ_t = (pr : ⌈log₂ max_{b′}|Pred_{t+1}(b′)|⌉ bits, data residues)`.
**[R2]** Revision 1 erased "the exit conditions" from `(pc′, pr)`. That is
false for raw predicates: an inactive block's predicate, or a redundant one,
is not a function of the edge taken. The repaired step uses *active one-hot
edge flags*. `Pred` ranges over edges `(b, e)`, including fault, success,
dynamic-target and HALT self-loop edges; ranks are injective per target.

Step `t`: (1) for `b ∈ Reach_t` compute `at_b = [pc = b]` (one-hot on the
reachable domain); (2) from the *old* data compute
`h_{b,e} = at_b ∧ [b takes exit e]` — exhaustive and mutually exclusive, so
exactly one is 1 — and uncompute every predicate helper while the old data is
still there; (3) apply each block's data path under its flags, leaving new
vars, counter, outcome and data residues; under `h_{b,e}`:
`pc′ ^= succ(b,e)`, `pr ^= rank_{t+1}(b,e)`; (4) XOR `decode_old(pc′, pr)`
into old `pc` (now clean); take `pc′` as `pc`; (5) XOR
`[decode_edge(pc, pr) = (b,e)]` into each `h_{b,e}` and
`[decode_old(pc, pr) = b]` into each `at_b`. HALT is an ordinary block with a
self-loop and no data path. Data-path helpers are *not* erased by the rank:
each is cleaned by its own local construction or is data residue.

**Lemma 5.6.** Step `t` is a sound move
`{σ_t} ⟶ {σ_{t+1}, ρ_t}` with all step scratch clean, where `σ_t, ρ_t` are
the named values "GBM state after `t` steps" and "frame of step `t`".

## 6. The chain game

After §5 the computation is a chain: values `σ_0 … σ_T` (width `m`), residues
`ρ_0 … ρ_{T−1}` (width `r`), step moves `{σ_j} ⟷ {σ_{j+1}, ρ_j}` of cost `a`,
and `Copy` on `σ_j` of cost `k`.

**Definition 6.1 (chain game `𝒞(T, m, r, a, k; S)`).** A configuration is
`(τ, E)`: a multiset `τ` of token positions in `{0..T}` and a set
`E ⊆ {0..T−1}` of retained residues. `space = m·|τ| + r·|E| ≤ S` always.
Moves: `Adv(j)`: a token `j → j+1`, requires `j ∉ E`, adds `j` to `E`, cost
`a`. `Ret(j)`: a token `j+1 → j`, requires `j ∈ E`, removes it, cost `a`.
`Dup(j)` / `Undup(j)`: add / remove a duplicate token at `j`, cost `k`.
Start `({0}, ∅)`. Goal: any configuration with a token at `T`.
`Time*(T; S)` is the minimum total cost; the oracle costs `2·Time* + commit`.
**[R2]** Conventions fixed by THEORY B.1: `Undup` needs two distinct physical
tokens at `j` and removes one (never the last); a residue is a shared named
value, usable by any token at the right position; both endpoints of every
move respect `S`; evaluator workspace is outside `S` and must be reserved
separately; `S` is fungible between tokens and residues.

**Theorem 6.2 (soundness).** Every legal play is a derivation in §3.
**[R2]** A legal play *that reaches a token at `T`*, where `σ_T` holds the
payload and code, yields a correct oracle by Theorem 2.5. (The empty play is
legal and computes nothing.) [mechanised: `Chain.legal_play_oracle`,
`Chain.checked_play_oracle`]

**Results on Problem 6.3** (THEORY Part B; details and proofs there).
- *Exact, unrestricted, small:* shortest path over configurations; exponential,
  used as ground truth.
- *Nested class `N`* (history leaves; "bridge" = run a sub-routine forward,
  copy its endpoint into a reserved bundle, run it backward): the interval
  recurrence `F(i,j;s) = min(history leaf, min_h 2F(i,h;s−m_h) + k_h + F(h,j;s−m_i))`
  is exactly optimal within `N`, uniform and non-uniform. It agreed with
  unrestricted search on all 623 uniform and 27 non-uniform instances checked.
  Whether `N` is optimal among all plays is **open**.
- *Capacity:* `R_N(S) = max_d 2^d ⌊(S − (d+1)m)/r⌋`; the unrestricted game
  cannot reach beyond `2^{K−1} − 1`, `K` the most tokens-plus-residues that
  fit in `S`.
- *Lower bound:* `Time* ≥ a·max(T, 2T − L) + k·[T > L]`, `L = ⌊(S−m)/r⌋`.
- *Compact schedules:* balanced `κ`-ary recursion of depth `L` with history
  leaves of length `ℓ`: `T = ℓκ^L`, cost `aℓ(2κ−1)^L`, peak
  `m(1 + L(κ−1)) + ℓr`; `O(L)` records. Binary fan-out (`κ = 2`) is THEORY
  B.6; general `κ` is `theory/kary_frontier.py`, within a factor 1.53 of the
  exact class-`N` optimum on the 85 small instances compared, and far better
  than binary at scale.

**Problem 6.3 (as posed in revision 1).**
(a) An exact recurrence for `Time*`, with proof of optimality within the game,
or a proof for a named strategy class plus the exhaustive-search evidence that
bounds the gap at small `T`.
(b) Closed forms: for `S = p·m + ℓ·r`, the largest `T` reachable and the time
to reach it; the two limits `r → 0` (history only, Bennett 1973: time `2T`,
space `m + rT`) and `ℓ = 1` (Bennett 1989 / Knill 1995).
(c) The optimal leaf length `ℓ*` as a function of `m/r` and the exchange rate
between a qubit and a step in the target objective.
(d) The non-uniform game (`m_j, r_j, a_j` varying): an exact DP for nested
strategies and its complexity; a representation of the optimal uniform
strategy in `O(log T)` space, so that the schedule for `T = 10⁹` is a few
dozen records.
(e) A lower bound on `Time*(T; S)` to certify the gap of any schedule found.

## 7. Cost and emission

**Definition 7.1 (hierarchical circuit).** Terms:
`gate | seq(c…) | call(def, wire-map) | adj(c) | ctrl(p, c) | rep(n, c, shift) | with(alloc n; c)`,
definitions shared. `rep` iterates a body with an affine wire shift.

**Theorem 7.2.** Gate counts, peak live wires and wire-time of a term are
computable by structural recursion over the *shared* term DAG, in time linear
in its size and independent of the expanded gate count; they equal the flat
circuit's. Flat emission is a streaming traversal using memory
`O(nesting depth × widest wire-map)`.

A compile whose term DAG has size `O(|program| + log B)` therefore prices and
emits a 10⁹-step oracle without holding it.
**[R2]** Whether a given oracle *admits* such a term is a property of the
oracle: it needs the reachability sets and residue widths to have a regular
summary. Computing `Reach_t` step by step is already `Θ(H)` work. The
requirement is a design goal met on regular programs, not a theorem about
every oracle.

## 7a. ISA obligations **[R2]**

The realisation is bound through §17 item 18 (a pebbled expansion library with
a full-domain argument and a complete, ordinal-numbered operation list). It is
*not* claimed that each erasure inside it is a §6 `reverse` cleanup: erasing a
value by an algebraic identity from an updated operand (an in-place update's
old value, the old pc from the predecessor rank) is a sound move but is not
the adjoint of that value's computing sequence on its original versions. §6
governs producer-written structured regions; circuit soundness and §6
annotation are separate conclusions.

## 8. Extensions stated, not yet in the core

- **Ghost pebbles** (measurement-based uncomputation; Gidney, Kornerup et al.):
  a third wire status `ghost(v)` carrying phase `(−1)^{m·⟦v⟧}`; rule
  `π + (R, v) ⟶ π + ghost(v)` by X-basis measurement, discharged by
  re-evaluating `v` and applying classically controlled `Z`. Leaves the
  permutation tier; ISA 0.9 does not admit it (§6.4). Soundness and the
  controlled form are owed before it enters the calculus.
- **Memory**: a region is a value per cell version; `store` at a classical
  address is `InPlace` exchange with residue the old cell; at a superposed
  address it is the same under a decoded-address guard per candidate cell
  (value-set analysis bounds the candidates).
- **Dirty borrowing**: frame rule on wires in unknown state, per §5.6.
